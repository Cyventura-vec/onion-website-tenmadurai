The consequences of Tenmadurai's indifference did not appear all at once.

There was no single day when the city declared its knowledge lost. No fire consumed the library. No invading army destroyed its archives. No great disaster swept through the streets.

Instead, the loss came quietly.

The scholars who had preserved Tenmadurai's knowledge grew older. The Street Academies became smaller. Their teachers continued their work, but there were fewer students willing to dedicate their lives to learning and fewer patrons willing to support those who taught it.

Eventually, the old scholars began to disappear.

With them went knowledge that had never been completely written down.

Stories once passed from teacher to student became fragmented. Traditional methods, observations, interpretations, and local histories were remembered differently by each generation. Without enough people capable of preserving and teaching them, the details gradually faded.

Within two generations, entire portions of Tenmadurai's oral tradition had become incomplete.

Some stories survived only as fragments.

Some names remained without their histories.

Some manuscripts could still be found, but there were no longer enough scholars capable of understanding them. Knowledge could survive on a shelf, yet become meaningless when nobody remained who knew how to interpret it.

The great library stood as the clearest symbol of this decline.

Its halls remained, but the voices that had once filled them were gone. Shelves that had once represented generations of accumulated knowledge slowly became monuments to what the city had failed to preserve.

Tenmadurai had not been conquered.

It had not been destroyed by famine, plague, war, or natural disaster.

It had simply stopped remembering.

The tragedy of Tenmadurai was therefore not the destruction of knowledge, but the gradual abandonment of it. Each generation inherited less than the one before. Each forgotten lesson made the next loss easier. Eventually, the people could no longer remember what they had once known.

And when the last scholars were gone, there was no one left to explain what had been lost.

The city did not fall to an enemy.

It forgot itself.

And with the last people who remembered, the knowledge of Tenmadurai perished with them.
